go back

i don't know if perfectionist is the right word, but i do set very high standards for myself. ones that i never meet, so i guess im kind of failing as a "perfectionist"

whenever i work on a project, i need it to be the best it can be. because if i dont put it all my effort how can i ever improve?

but the thing is, i don't have enough energy or motivation. so it always ends up being lackluster. and as a result, i'm very hard on myself. on the fact that i can't get myself to work on whatever it is, and the fact that it's not my best work.

i think its part of the reason why i changed my website so much in the beginning. my layouts emphasized drawn assets, and i felt like i could always improve on them, so i kept making iterations.

i got tired. so i settled on a simple, minimalist design that wouldn't drain me to upkeep. which is good, i don't feel as stressed anymore, but i do get a little self-conscious of it being so mediocre compared to others on the indie web.

it's just not it in me to give it my all everytime, so sometimes i feel like i shouldn't even bother, but that's very counterproductive.

i guess i don't know if i know how to be more.. relaxed?? about my work. i recently heard the phrase "C's get degrees" from my friend. and it's stupid but it kind of opened my eyes a bit. i felt terrible but i submitted an essay draft that was 1/3rd of the word requirement- and got a C on it. i was genuinely so surprised i got that high of a grade.

i was always encouraged to get all A's. it's not like i was ever punished for bad grades but they were less desired. but i never allowed myself to get anything lower than a B anyways (unless it was math, which i struggled with) so i don't know how my parents would react if i got lower grades. pre-highschool i was a "gifted" kid LOL so i guess that explains some things.

but i don't know if i can continue to have the expectation of "giving it my all" everytime anymore, as depressing as that sounds. but i guess it doesn't have to be a bad thing..! maybe i just need to learn to deligate my energy. save it for the things that matter.
